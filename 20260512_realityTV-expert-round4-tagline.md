## 2. What we're commissioning

The brand wordmark for trailers + doubland.ai + future cohort marketing will be a **two-line lockup**:

```
       DOUBLAND        ← H1 (brand name, locked)
       <tagline>       ← H2 (we need your help)
```

H1 is the brand name — locked. H2 is **3–7 words** that sits below the brand name in every trailer's opening splash card and on the doubland.ai homepage.

This isn't a billboard slogan. It's a **branded tagline that becomes inseparable from the brand name itself** — said in the same breath. Think:

- *"It's Not TV. It's HBO."* (HBO — denial + brand insertion)
- *"Outwit. Outplay. Outlast."* (Survivor — format formula)
- *"Expect the unexpected."* (Big Brother — promise of unpredictability)
- *"See what's next."* (Netflix — anticipation)

The goal: when someone sees the tagline alone, they think Doubland. When they see Doubland alone, they hear the tagline.

## 3. What the tagline needs to do

Three jobs, in priority order:

### Job 1 — Imprint in memory after one viewing
Watch the v2.1 opener once → remember Doubland *and* the tagline 24 hours later. Stickiness is the primary metric. We're at the **first-time viewer → daily habit** funnel step from Round 1; the tagline has to survive that 24-hour gap.

### Job 2 — Hint at the format DNA
The tagline alone (no other context) should suggest:
- This is a competition format with personal stakes
- It involves "Doubles" (the brand-specific word — leans on the H1 above it)
- Something happens that you'd want to watch

We're NOT asking the tagline to explain the format. We're asking it to **bait curiosity** so a stranger seeing it on a thumbnail clicks.

### Job 3 — Optionally inspire action
The aspirational upside: the tagline should make viewers wonder what *their* Double would do. The product roadmap includes letting viewers commission their own Double for future cohorts. The tagline doesn't have to *say* this, but the best taglines plant the seed.

Reality TV taglines that achieve Job 3:
- *"Find out what happens when people stop being polite and start getting real"* — The Real World — explicitly invites projection
- *"Could you make it?"* — Survivor early seasons — direct viewer challenge

---

## 4. Constraints

- **3–7 words.** Shorter is stickier. Three-beat formula (X. Y. Z.) is acceptable; full sentences are acceptable; question form is acceptable.
- **No "Doubles" or "Doubland" inside the tagline** — those are H1 and bake naturally into the cohort vocabulary. The tagline should work standalone.
- **No urgency framing.** Round 3 advisor banned this for the end card; same rule applies here. No "Don't miss it" / "Before it's too late" / "Limited slots."
- **No fully aspirational corporate copy** (e.g. "Where stories live", "Reality, reimagined"). Sounds like a fragrance ad.
- **No game-show copy** (e.g. "Who will survive?", "One winner takes all"). The format already does this through the end card's "Who will stay alive?"; the tagline should NOT echo it.
- **Must work in both lowercase and small caps typesetting** — the designer is locking the typography against this content, so taglines that read awkwardly in caps (e.g. punctuation-heavy) are out.

## 5. Some directions we've tried (so you can rule them out or steer)

Our internal candidates so far, none of which feel right yet:

- *"Where Doubles live their best lives."* — too cheery, doesn't capture the survival/competition register
- *"Real lives. Played by AI."* — accurate but flat; reads like a product description
- *"Same people. Different choices."* — already in use as the dramatic frame from Round 2; could double-duty as the tagline?
- *"Your social self. Played to the extreme."* — captures the format but feels self-help-conference-y
- *"Watch your double live differently."* — passive; doesn't earn the brand promise
- *"Where instincts have consequences."* — abstract; doesn't suggest competition
- *"The version of you that doesn't sleep."* — intriguing but creepy; verges on horror
- *"Real people. AI doubles. One winner."* — too formula-driven; reads like a description on a streamer

**The pattern of failure:** we keep landing on either *too descriptive* (sounds like a product blurb) or *too abstract* (sounds like a perfume ad). The Reality TV register — confident, intimate, slightly conspiratorial — is what we keep missing.

## 6. The seven questions

Please respond with **5–10 candidate taglines** + one recommended pick. Brief reasoning per candidate is more useful than long explanations. Cover as many of these angles as your experience suggests:

1. **"Same people. New pressure. Different choices."** is already approved as our active dramatic frame from Round 2. Can it pull double-duty as the tagline (H2) below DOUBLAND, or does the brand H2 need a fundamentally different register?

2. **Three-beat formula** (X. Y. Z.) feels brand-appropriate for our cinematic register. Are there three-beat candidates that work for our format? (Survivor's *"Outwit. Outplay. Outlast"* is the gold standard. What's our version that doesn't sound like a Survivor knockoff?)

3. **Question vs. statement.** The end card already has a question ("Who will stay alive?"). Should the tagline mirror that interrogative register, or contrast it with a confident statement?

4. **First-person voice ("you", "your") vs. third-person.** First-person taglines (e.g. *"Find out what your Double would do"*) invite projection. Third-person taglines (e.g. *"Reality. Doubled."*) establish brand. Which works better for the funnel we're building (viewer → daily habit → eventually cohort member)?

5. **The "act" angle.** Can a tagline plausibly hint at the participation upside (commission your own Double) without being a CTA? Or is that too much work for 3–7 words?

6. **Length sweet spot.** We've assumed 3–7 words. Are there competition-format brands where a single-word tagline worked (e.g. just "Survive."), or is that too thin?

7. **Cultural reference test.** Once you propose your top 2–3, imagine each tagline said aloud on a podcast: "Have you seen the new show, Doubland — [tagline]?" Which one survives that test? Which one makes the listener ask "what is that?"
