# methodology.md
> A practical, comprehensive method to assess personality and turn it into a usable “soul.md”.
> Designed for self-use and to help friends (with consent). Not a clinical diagnosis tool.

---

## 0) Core principles
1. **Triangulate**: self-report + behavior + other people’s observations.
2. **Predict in contexts**: “If–then” beats labels.
3. **Weight behavior**: what you do matters more than what you say you do.
4. **Separate stable vs situational**: traits vs current stress/job/relationship phase.
5. **Make it testable**: predictions must be validated against real events.
6. **Ethics first**: consent, privacy, non-manipulation.

---

## 1) Outputs you will produce
- **soul.md**: your final snapshot used to train the agent.
- **evidence.md** (optional): raw notes, quotes, examples, and links to incidents.
- **scenarios.md** (optional): expanded “if–then” situations with expected reactions.
- **change_log.md** (optional): what changed and why.

---

## 2) The 6-layer model (what you’re measuring)
1. **Traits**: stable tendencies (Big Five / HEXACO style).
2. **Motives & values**: what you chase/avoid; what matters most.
3. **Interpersonal style**: trust, boundaries, attachment leaning, conflict pattern.
4. **Stress profile**: triggers, regulation, recovery, threat sensitivity.
5. **Decision style**: planning horizon, risk tolerance, ambiguity tolerance.
6. **Context modifiers**: sleep, health, work pressure, relationships, environment.

---

## 3) Data collection methods (do all if possible)

### 3.1 Self-report questionnaires (fast baseline)
Pick 1–2 for traits and 1 for values.
- **Traits** (choose one):
  - Big Five (e.g., BFI-2 / IPIP-NEO style)
  - HEXACO (adds Honesty–Humility)
- **Values**:
  - Schwartz values-style ranking (or a simple top-10 values list)
- **Optional**:
  - Grit / self-control measure
  - Conflict style questionnaire
  - Attachment-style screener

**Rule:** treat results as *signals*, not truth.

### 3.2 Structured interview with yourself (or with a friend)
Use consistent prompts; write answers in plain language.

Core prompts:
- “When do I feel most like myself?”
- “What do I protect at all costs?”
- “What reliably makes me angry?”
- “What do I do when I’m overwhelmed?”
- “What patterns show up in my relationships?”
- “What do I regret repeating?”
- “What do I admire in others (and why)?”

### 3.3 Behavioral evidence log (highest value)
Collect 20–40 examples from the last 12 months.
For each event, record:
- Situation (who/where/what)
- What you felt + thought
- What you did (observable)
- Outcome
- What you’d do again / change

This becomes your grounding dataset.

### 3.4 Informant feedback (very high value)
Ask 3 people who see you in different roles:
- close friend/partner
- coworker/peer
- family member or long-time friend

Use the same 10 questions so answers are comparable:
1. My biggest strengths are…
2. My blind spots are…
3. Under stress I tend to…
4. In conflict I tend to…
5. What reliably motivates me is…
6. What reliably demotivates me is…
7. How I show care is…
8. What I avoid is…
9. What’s a pattern I repeat?
10. Advice you’d give “future me”?

**Consent + privacy:** tell them what will be stored and who sees it.

### 3.5 Situational judgment tests (turns personality into prediction)
Create 12–20 realistic scenarios and force choices.
Examples:
- someone cancels last minute
- public criticism
- friend asks for help when you’re busy
- unfair blame at work
- unexpected expense
- flirtation / mixed signals
- teammate underperforming

For each, choose:
- likely action (A/B/C)
- your internal story (“what it means”)
- what you’d say out loud
- what you’d do if you were your best self

### 3.6 Real-world tracking (2–4 weeks)
Lightweight daily check-in (2 minutes):
- Energy (0–10)
- Stress (0–10)
- Social desire (0–10)
- One meaningful event + reaction
- One thing you avoided
- Sleep hours

This identifies context modifiers.

## 4) Scoring and synthesis (how you turn raw data into soul.md)

### 4.1 Extract “behavioral rules”
From your evidence log + scenarios, write rules like:
- “If I feel disrespected in public, I become more direct and less patient.”
- “If I have time to prepare, I’m calm; if surprised, I’m sharper.”

Aim for 30–60 rules. Later you’ll compress to the most predictive 10–20.

### 4.2 Build the profile statements (short, testable)
Convert traits into *consequences*:
- Instead of “high conscientiousness,” write:
  - “I plan ahead, I dislike last-minute changes, and I judge myself by follow-through.”

### 4.3 Identify triggers + regulators
Make a table:
- Trigger → typical reaction → best intervention → worst intervention

### 4.4 Resolve contradictions (very common)
When you do “opposite things,” it usually means context is the switch.
Write:
- “I’m X when Y; I’m Z when not-Y.”

Examples:
- “I’m outgoing with friends, quiet in new groups.”
- “I’m calm with time, reactive when rushed.”

### 4.5 Confidence levels
For each major claim in soul.md, tag:
- **High**: repeated behavior + informant agrees
- **Medium**: some examples, mixed feedback
- **Low**: mostly self-belief, not tested

---

## 5) Validation loop (how you know it works)
Every week for 4 weeks:
1. Pick 2 predictions from soul.md (e.g., “how I handle criticism”).
2. Watch real situations.
3. Mark:
   - hit / partial / miss
4. Update rules and triggers.

Goal: increase hit rate, not perfect prediction.

---

## 6) How to help a friend (responsibly)
### 6.1 Consent script (simple)
- “I can help you build a personality snapshot for self-understanding and communication. You control what’s written and who sees it.”

### 6.2 Boundaries
- Don’t label disorders.
- Don’t use it to manipulate partners/employees.
- Keep their doc private by default.
- Let them own final wording.

### 6.3 Friend workflow (90 minutes total)
1. 10 min: goals + situations to predict
2. 15 min: quick trait + values prompts
3. 25 min: 6 key stories (conflict, rejection, pressure, success, failure, change)
4. 20 min: 6 scenarios forced-choice
5. 20 min: write their v0.1 soul.md + 10 predictions

---

## 7) Recommended update cadence
- **Weekly (2 min):** add one new behavior example
- **Monthly (15 min):** update triggers, rules, confidence
- **Quarterly (45 min):** rerun scenarios, compare predictions vs reality
- **After major life events:** do a focused update (context modifiers change fast)

---

## 8) Common failure modes to avoid
- Writing a “nice biography” instead of predictive rules.
- Using flattering labels, ignoring ugly patterns.
- Overfitting to one recent event.
- Treating self-report as truth without behavioral proof.
- Forgetting context switches (sleep, stress, power dynamics).

---

## 9) Minimal starter plan (if you want the fastest path)
Day 1 (60–90 min):
- Fill soul.md sections 0–8 roughly.
- Write 10 calibration scenarios (section 11).
Week 1:
- Log 10 real events + reactions.
- Ask 2 informants the 10 questions.
Week 2:
- Update soul.md with rules + triggers + confidence.
