# Growth and income — research inquiry

**To:** Growth and consumer-monetization experts who have shipped a product whose fun depends on a real group, and whose variable cost is not zero. Relevant pattern knowledge: party games, cohort products, reality-TV fandom, creator memberships, and AI products that pay for generation per use. Feed and ad-network experience is not the fit.

**From:** Ivan Pistsov, founder — Doubland (https://www.doubland.ai)

**Date:** 2026-10-14

**Ask:** Read this and return one income architecture for the twelve months after our first public season. Push back on the hypotheses below. We want a decision we can execute, with a price band, a payer, a sequence, and a kill line.

**Time:** About 90 minutes. Two pages back is enough. A menu of twelve ideas is a failed answer.

---

## 1. The decision we want

Doubland is about to run its first public season. That season exists to produce fundraising metrics: retention, pull, and named people who ask to host the show for their own group.

Separately, the company needs a way to earn money from the same product if venture money is slow or never arrives.

We need you to choose the income system that can grow beside that season, and to say what must stay unbuilt until the season has spoken.

A good answer has all of these:

1. One recommended stack for the next twelve months. Name the payer, what stays free, the first thing we sell, and the second thing we sell.
2. One serious alternative you rejected, and the reason.
3. A keep / change / kill mark on each hypothesis in section 6.
4. What we measure during the free season, and what we must not test because it would spoil the fundraising read.
5. A kill line: the result that means we stop the recommended path.

---

## 2. What Doubland is

Doubland is a private show about you and your friends.

A person takes a short personality quiz and receives a Double: an AI character that acts from their answers. The Double lives in a shared simulated town with the Doubles of people they already know. It wakes, works, talks, and, in the current format, competes. The person does not have to play. They watch. Each day the system cuts a short episode of what the group’s Doubles did.

The product is six things in one:

1. A social product whose unit is people you already know.
2. A reality show about that group, with a new episode every day.
3. A game that looks like a day of life, watchable in a few minutes.
4. A mirror: “that’s so them.”
5. A safe place to practice a different word or move and see the scene play out.
6. A place to play out a big choice before making it. We do not claim prediction.

The atomic network is one host and one real group. We call the host a **Showrunner**: the person who brings a named group, gets them through the quiz, and holds the season. Without a Showrunner the town is an empty map. Viewers and cast members are the easy side. Density inside one group matters more than a large audience of strangers.

Audience today: adults 18 and over, in invite-only groups we can actually run. We do not target, market to, or collect data from anyone under 18.

Public line for the mirror: “See yourself from the outside.” We do not say “coach.” We do not sell personality data.

## 3. What is true right now

**The first public season is LeaderTalks (L-Talks).**

- One existing Telegram group of a few hundred adults. Ivan is Showrunner number one.
- The cast is 15 Doubles, already built, in a Downtown Pittsburgh town.
- The format on screen is Survival: a shared day, a challenge, an evening vote, someone leaves. The season shape is a premiere plus 15 evening episodes. Night 15 names a winner.
- Watching is free. Joining this season as a viewer is free. Showrunners are not paid, and we are not offering them cash.
- A door already exists on the site: “Run Doubland for Your Group.” A submit is interest. It is not an activated group.
- The simulation engine for this season is locked and running. Automatic trailer generation is in progress and is not yet a consumer product with a price.
- No public revenue. No retention curve yet. Do not invent traction, and do not assume willingness to pay.

**What a viewer gets on the free show**

- One evening episode for the whole group, on the order of 60 to 120 seconds, vertical, one per simulated day.
- A live and replay view of the town.
- A person card for each Double.

**What we believe is the paid object, and have not sold**

- A short personal cut of one Double’s day (“your Double’s day”), on the order of 15 to 60 seconds, generated from a day that already happened.
- A second ruleset besides Survival. The example in our heads is a status-and-money game in the vein of a property-trading board game. That example is an illustration. It is not a licensed title, and it is not built.

**What is designed and not built**

- A solo scene: one person, one short pre-made situation, one clip back, then a second take if they ask the Double to play it differently.
- A one-player sandbox: your Double alone in a small space while you wait for friends.
- A standing public world that a stranger can move into. We are calling this the **master simulation** in this brief. It does not exist. LeaderTalks is a closed cast, not that world.

**The only solo path that exists today** is “bring your people and become a Showrunner.”

**An earlier internal draft** priced the subscription as always-on compute: your Double runs while your device is open for free, and you pay for 24/7 hosting and a larger budget. That draft is not the current intent. The current intent is in section 5. If the cost envelope forces a return to metering, say so. Keep any meter inside the product. Do not put a compute invoice on the front door.

## 4. The economic fact

Generation cost scales with how many Doubles think, and for how many minutes. It does not scale with how many people watch.

Planning envelope, written June 2026 against an OpenAI bill, for a town of about 15 Doubles:

- About $2 to $7 of model cost for one fully watched simulated day.
- About $60 to $200 a month if that town runs every day.
- A $10 to $30 monthly price on a private always-on town was, on that envelope, several times underwater.
- A later cost program aims at roughly $5 to $15 a month per private town. That program is not done.
- The live LeaderTalks run uses a cheaper model than that June bill. Treat the range above as the conservative case. We have not re-measured this season for this brief.
- Voice, image, and edit for a trailer are extra. We do not have a stable dollar cost per personalized minute. Leave a blank, and tell us the maximum trailer cost you would accept as a share of its price.

One shared town amortizes across every viewer. A private town per person, or per friend group, does not. A personal daily film can only be cut from a day that something already simulated. Selling the film does not remove the cost of the day.

We can run a small number of towns well. A fleet of always-on private worlds is not the next two quarters.

## 5. Founder locks for this inquiry

Treat these as fixed unless you believe one of them makes a workable income system impossible. If you break one, name it, name the metric it damages, and name the replacement.

1. **The LeaderTalks season stays free.** Nobody in that Telegram group pays to watch, to be in the cast, or to claim a Double. We will not A/B a paywall on those 15 nights.
2. **Participation in a friend-group season stays free for the members.** Income comes from something a person is glad to add, not from unlocking their own Double.
3. **The magic is the group that already knows each other.** A world of strangers is allowed only as a bridge into that, and only if it does not become the product people settle for.
4. **Adults only.** No campus-as-the-lead, no minor audience, no personality-data business, no dark-pattern upgrade path.
5. **Showrunners stay unpaid through this season.** Status, tools, and recognition first. Any cash, stipend, or revenue share is a later legal question. Do not make “pay the hosts” part of the twelve-month recommendation.
6. **Fundraising metrics stay clean.** The season must still be able to show: people who watched night 1 are still there on later nights; people ask for more without being prompted; one to three named people ask to host their own group; members say “that’s so them.” Targets we are judging ourselves against, not results: about 60% back the next day, about 40% after a week, about 25% after a month, as the “good” band for a consumer social app at seed. Small N is acceptable. Vanity view counts are not.
7. **We would rather grow slowly on revenue than distort this season to look fundable.**

Pitch targets above are bars, not data. Honest zeros after the season are acceptable. Inventing a payer is not.

## 6. Hypotheses to mark keep, change, or kill

These are the founder’s current beliefs. They are not a plan you have to bless.

**H1. Watching, and having your Double in a friend-group season, is free.**

**H2. The first thing people will pay for is a personal daily film** of their own Double, sold as a subscription to people who want that cut every day.

**H3. The second thing people will pay for is a custom scenario:** Survival is the first ruleset; further rulesets (the status-and-money example, and others) are paid add-ons.

**H4. Someone with no friend group still needs a way in,** or we lose them. The two candidates are a solo game, and joining an ongoing master simulation made of people who do not know each other.

**H5. Those solo and master-sim users will pay a premium subscription,** even though the better experience is the friend group, and even though their path is a worse version of the core product.

**H6. This income system can be stood up without a venture round,** on the cost envelope in section 4, with a handful of groups rather than a million users.

---

## 7. The three shapes we can actually tell apart

Please pick one, or replace all three with a shape you like better. Do not blend them into a hybrid unless the payer and the free line stay obvious.

**Shape A — The group pays for a season. Members do not.**

The free public object is one house show (LeaderTalks, then later house shows we produce). A second group gets a season because a Showrunner, or the group as a whole, pays for that season. Members watch free. The personal daily cut is an add-on only for someone whose Double is already inside a season that is running. A solo experience, if it exists, is one short scene that ends by asking for two friends. Strangers do not move into the house show.

**Shape B — The individual pays to live somewhere.**

A person with no group puts their Double into a solo game or into a master simulation, and pays a subscription. The subscription includes the personal daily film and access to extra scenarios. Friend-group play stays free and remains the better product. This is the founder’s H4 plus H5.

**Shape C — Viewers pay for a richer cut of the one house show.**

We keep a single produced world. Income is a membership or a tip for extra episodes, a personal cut of a Double who already lives in that world, or a season pass to the public show. We do not spin up a private town per customer until the daily cost of a private town sits inside its price.

## 8. Questions

Answer inside the recommendation. A separate essay per question is not required.

**Q1. Who pays, for what, and what never has a price?**

We need one sentence a founder can say when asked how Doubland makes money. Then the stack.

**Q2. Is the personal daily film the right first sale?**

Consider that the film is worthless without a simulated day, that daily cadence multiplies render cost, and that the free group episode already exists. If you keep it: who is allowed to buy it, what cadence you would actually sell (every day, a few a week, or a pack for the season), a price band in US dollars, and the maximum render cost as a share of that price. If you kill it: what replaces it as the first sale.

**Q3. Who pays for a second ruleset, if anyone?**

Survival is the live format. A further scenario can be a subscription feature, a per-season purchase, or something we do not sell this year. Say who the buyer is. A Showrunner buying a scenario for their group is a different product from a lonely subscriber unlocking a scenario for themselves.

**Q4. Does a master simulation help the friend-group network, or replace it?**

If you recommend one: who is allowed to live there, whether residence is free, what a spectator gets, how a resident graduates into a real group, and the rule that stops the master sim from becoming the thing people settle for. If you reject one: say what the person with no friends does on day one instead. “Come back when you have friends” is an acceptable answer if you defend it.

Pillar we already hold: a world of strangers is secondary, and is allowed when it helps someone reach a real group and does not break the feeling that this show is about people you know.

**Q5. Can a solo experience carry a subscription?**

The built-but-not-shipped version is one short scene and a clip, not an always-on life. Tell us whether that object is a free hook, a one-time purchase, or a subscription. If it is a subscription, say what the second month is, because a single scene does not fill a month.

**Q6. What do we learn during the free LeaderTalks season?**

Give at most five measurements or offers. They must leave the 15 nights, the free watch, and the Showrunner door intact. Separate a signal that helps the raise from a signal that helps pricing. Name anything you want us to avoid because it would teach the cohort to wait for a discount, or would make retention unreadable.

The Showrunner door is already live. You may keep it as one of the five. You do not need to invent a second “host a group” form.

**Q7. What belongs in a seed conversation, and what stays off the deck?**

After this season we will talk to investors. Say which income facts, if any, make that conversation stronger, and which facts we should not claim until a second group has paid. One sentence for the room. One sentence we should refuse to say.

**Q8. What do we not build for six months?**

A short list. Include the shape you rejected. Be specific: a solo subscription app, a stranger town, a scenario store, a usage meter on the landing page, ads, and a cash program for Showrunners are all in bounds for that list.

**Q9. If you break the free-participation lock, do it here.**

State what you would charge, who pays, the price band, and which of the season’s metrics you are willing to damage. If you keep the lock, one line is enough.

## 9. What a usable price looks like

Use US dollars. Use bands, not false precision. For each paid offer in your stack:

| Offer | Who pays | Price band | When it can be sold | Cost it must clear |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | During LeaderTalks / when group two starts / only after private-town cost is inside the price | The June envelope, the cheaper live model, or trailer render |

Also say the smallest number of paying buyers that covers one private town for a month, at your price, on the conservative envelope. If that number is larger than a real friend group, the offer is a group purchase or it waits.

## 10. Kill line

For the path you recommend, give:

- The LeaderTalks result that means “do not build this.”
- The result 60 days after the first real charge that means “stop and switch.”
- The result that means “this can fund the next group without a round.”

We will treat a kill line we wrote down as seriously as a feature we shipped.

---

## 11. Leave these out

- A fundraising narrative, a deck, or invented metrics.
- A media plan for the Telegram group. The season’s job is already defined.
- Legal advice on paying Showrunners, entity structure, or securities.
- A recommendation to market to minors, to schools, or to a campus tour as the lead.
- A recommendation to sell, share, or research-license personality data.
- Engine, model, or infrastructure design. The constraint you have is section 4. The remedy for a cost problem, in this brief, is what we sell and when, not a new architecture.
- Trademarked game titles as products we should ship. “A status-and-money scenario” is as specific as we want.
