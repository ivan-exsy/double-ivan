# ReelShort as a model for personal "day in the life" Double episodes
COS synthesis, 2026-10-01. Sources: research.md (ReelShort research, cited) and specialist-inputs.md (Screenwriter, Reality TV, Jordan Peterson, Monetization, Andrew Chen, PLG, Engagement, Video Producer).

## Bottom line
Borrow ReelShort's craft (3-second hook, 60-90s vertical episodes, a cliffhanger at the end of every episode, a free taste before paying). Don't copy its money machine (coin unlocks at every cliffhanger, weekly auto-renew at $19.99, daily check-in rewards, paid ads to strangers). ReelShort made $785M in 2025, but it spends about 55% of revenue on ads and keeps only about 2% of users by day 30. Doubland's edge is that the audience is a friend group, which costs almost nothing to acquire.

## What ReelShort does (key facts)
- $785M revenue in 2025, $1.05B projected for 2026; 286M downloads; US users watch 35.7 min/day.
- Episodes 1 to about 8-13 are free; after that roughly $0.18-0.60 per episode in coins, or VIP at $19.99/week. A full series runs $10-20+.
- The paywall sits right after a plot reversal. Retention: about 27% at day 1, under 10% at day 7 (when the free episodes run out), about 2% at day 30.
- Complaints: opaque and rising prices, charges after cancelling. AI production now cuts costs by up to 90%.

## Recommended format (team consensus)
- Your Double's side of the night the group already saw, not a separate show. The nightly closer stays the main show; nothing is revealed before the closer airs.
- 9:16, 60-120s sized to the day; a mute 3s cold open on the day's peak moment; one person, one real choice; a cliffhanger only on a thread the sim really left open (a vote not yet cast, an unanswered message, tomorrow's plan). Quiet days get a short day note, not padded drama.
- Third-person warm narrator using the Double's name, never "you." No narrated feelings: only what the ledger shows (vote reasons, last words, choices).
- Built around the friend group: friends who appear watch their own scene free and get an invite to claim their Double (Andrew Chen's loop). Never paywall a friend's view of themselves or the share clip.
- Release one each morning about yesterday, ending by pointing at tonight's vote. At most one push per day product-wide.

## Guardrails (non-negotiable)
Private by default; the owner previews before anything is shared; other Doubles only if opted in, otherwise blurred; fact-lock to the ledger; no villain edit, mocking, or humiliation; health, sexuality, private messages, and family go to approval; never cliffhang on the person's own fears (breakup, firing); "that's not me, fix it" is always free; clear prices, easy cancel, no loot-box mechanics; legal review of real-person likeness before launch.

## Money (open decision)
Team disagreement:
- Monetization: subscription first (Watch $12.99/mo, Producer $19.99-29.99/mo, season pass $9.99), web-first to avoid Apple's 30%; one free episode, then subscribe or invite a friend for another free day.
- Research and Engagement: sell a day or a weekly season as one purchase with no auto-renew ($0.99 per episode, $3.99-4.99 per season pass), no per-cliffhanger unlocks.
- PLG: no paywall until the resident has shared at least one clip; run a no-paywall holdout to make sure paying doesn't reduce sharing.
- Cost is the deciding factor: fully AI-generated video costs about $4.50-10 per 90s episode (more with retries), about $40-135 per subscriber per month if daily. Rendering from the sim engine with templates, TTS voice, and 1-2 generated hero shots costs about $0.50-3. Only the render route makes the money work.
- Reminder: your current default is no monetization this chapter. Test the format free first.

## Suggested next steps
1. Prototype: Video Producer builds 3 personal episodes from one real sim night with a real fork, using the current trailer kit.
2. Test with those 3 people and their friends: would they watch it to the end, send it, and want tomorrow's? Measure real cost and render time per episode.
3. Only then pick a price model (a season pass vs subscription test, plus the no-paywall holdout) and send the likeness terms to Legal.

KPIs: 30%+ of residents watch their episode; completion 60%+; next-day closer viewing among watchers at or above non-watchers; closer share rate down no more than 10%; clip share rate; invites from episodes; opt-outs and complaints.
