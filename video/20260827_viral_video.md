## 1. What we are trying to do

Doubland is a village of Doubles (personality twins of real people). People can open a live **2D top-down view** of that village (the Phaser view). That view is powerful: you can zoom any moment, any talk, any Double. It is not entertaining to sit in. Only a determined person stays there.

The **trailer** is the thing people enjoy and forward. Its job is to carry the a-ha: an unexpected turn, a dramatic beat, a personal discovery.

A second job sits on top of that: **teach people to see a real-life scene when they later look at the 2D village.** If they only remember a movie, they cannot read the live view. If they only see the map, they will not watch.

Long term, the live world may become real 3D. That is not today. Do not cut as if the live room is already a movie. Do not pretend the 2D view is fun.

---

## 2. The recommendation (one page)

**Most of the trailer is cinematic** (realistic rendered pictures). About 80% of a ~60 second group tape.

**2D is not the show.** It is a short flash of the *same beat* so the eye learns: that little figure is that leaving, that talk, that discovery.

**Three 2D beats, same order, every group daily:**

1. **Plant** — 2–3 seconds, silent, top-down. Who is where. Not a tour.
2. **Cost dive** — the one required 2D → cinematic move, on the beat that costs someone (or the Peak if tonight has no Cost). Start on the 2D verb (the walk-out, the look), then the pixels become the lived moment, while the same voiceover sentence finishes.
3. **Door** — last 2 seconds, 2D again, then an end card with a written timestamp list. That list is how a determined person jumps into the 2D archive to inspect. It is not “the rest of the episode.”

Everything else is a **hard cut** inside the cinematic pictures. Do **not** blend 2D and cinematic on every scene change. That turns the trick into the content, and a nightly auto-cut cannot hold it.

**Zero 2D in the moving picture = fail** (a movie with no mapping).  
**2D as the body of the tape = fail** (people will not watch).

**Group tape now:** one shared show for the Telegram room. 1–2–3 Doubles. One turn, not the whole day. Target ~60 seconds (band 50–75, hard cap 90). Voiceover is one person’s want, the pressure, the turn, the cost, an unfinished question. The Door is a look, not a spoken “go watch.”

**Share clip (Spark):** 12–20 seconds, muted-safe, cropped from the cinematic a-ha. **No 2D in the share clip.** Mapping is the daily’s job. A map flash in 12 seconds looks like a glitch and will not get forwarded.

**Timestamps:** written list next to the tape (end card or pinned comment). Each line is who / what / sim-clock time, fact-locked to the ledger. Voiceover never reads the list. Tap (when engineering can deep-link) opens the 2D view paused on that Double at that moment. If we cannot deep-link, do not fake tappable stamps — write the list anyway as a receipt.

**Paid personal day-in-life (next, not today):** one sim day can yield N+1 tapes (one group + one per paying Double). Each payer is the lead of their own show. Same facts as the group tape, camera locked on one life. **Does not replace the group tape.** Same 2D grammar, more surgical (their mark on the map, their cost). “Day-in-life” is a billing name for one person’s scar, not a license to narrate breakfast-to-bed.

## 3. Why not pure cinematic, and why not 2D-first

Pure cinematic is enjoyable and shareable. It does not teach the village. After the movie, the live 2D view looks cheap.

2D-first is honest about the live product. People will not sit through it unless they are already determined. The a-ha dies.

The blend that works is **cinematic body + rare 2D legend.** The legend is paired: same blocking, same bodies, same verb. Not wallpaper 2D on every cut.

Two different uses of 2D, do not collapse them:

- **Seeing (in the tape):** feel a 2D room become a lived moment, then find that room again at the Door. That is how they later watch live 2D and recognize a scene.
- **Archive (the list):** a determined person jumps and inspects. A still on a card is a receipt. It does not replace the three beats in the tape.

## 4. How much 2D (numbers the team can cut to)

For a **~60 second group daily:**

| Beat | Picture | Time |
|------|---------|------|
| Plant | 2D overhead / flyover | 2.0–2.5s |
| A-ha | cinematic (the turn people enjoy) | ~45–50s |
| Cost dive | 2D verb → cinematic of that verb | 0.6–0.8s transition, inside the Cost sentence |
| Optional land | cinematic → 2D after Cost, only if you need “this is that figure” before the Door | 1.0–1.5s |
| Door | 2D tease + end card with timestamps | last 2.0s |

**Hard cap: 12 seconds of 2D in the whole tape. Target: 8–10 seconds.**  
Never sit in 2D more than 3 seconds without a dive starting. 2D is not a scene.

If you need more than 12 seconds of 2D, you have too many turns. Cut a beat. Do not add map.

**Share clip (12–20s):** all cinematic. One a-ha. One timestamp implied by the daily, not drawn on the clip.

## 5. Visual mechanics — 2D to cinematic (and back)

Name the moves once:

- **Dive in:** 2D top-down becomes the cinematic moment. This is the mapping.
- **Fracture out / land:** cinematic moment lands back on the 2D map. Receipt.
- **Hard cut:** no blend. New angle inside the same turn, or any change that is not an a-ha boundary.

### Dive in (required once)

**When:** the Cost starts (who paid tonight). If the ledger has no Cost, use the Peak. Never on scenery. Never on a second morph after the turn has already landed.

**Nightly recipe (auto-cut can do this):**

1. 2D capture of the verb (leave, look, sit, turn away). Names readable. No product UI.
2. About 12 frames: camera pushes in toward the body that acts.
3. About 8 frames: dissolve onto the cinematic of the **same verb**, same blocking.
4. Total transition: 0.6–0.8 seconds at 30 frames per second.
5. Voiceover does not pause. The Cost sentence started on 2D and finishes on cinematic.

**Gold / new type only:** the *first* dive of a hero cut may let pixels expand into the lived moment (about 20–28 frames). Later dives go back to the nightly recipe. One showcase morph per gold piece. Not every transition. Not Tuesday night.

**Skip the dive when:**

- the next shot is more coverage of the *same* a-ha — stay cinematic, hard cut
- they walk across the room inside one event — stay cinematic
- there is no 2D capture at that sim time — hard cut to cinematic, still write the timestamp, do not fake a plant

**Illegal:** 2D → pretty cinematic of a *different* action, with no timestamp. Texture-morph as the whole cut.

### Fracture out / land (Door required; mid-tape optional)

**When:** leaving the a-ha for the Door. Optional once after the Cost cinematic if the team needs the blob labeled before the Door.

**Nightly recipe:**

1. About 10 frames: snap-zoom out to the 2D capture that matches last blocking.
2. Hold 1.0–2.0 seconds of 2D (Door tease).
3. End card: rows of time | who | what. Same rows as the archive seek.

**Illegal:** cinematic → raw 2D with no names or time (looks like a graphics drop). Fracture “just to remind them 2D exists.” Gagging someone’s cost by bouncing to cute 2D.

### Hard cut (default)

**When:** cinematic → cinematic inside one a-ha. Any scene change that is **not** plant, Cost, or Door.

**Founder asked for 2D↔3D on some or all scene changes. Answer: some.** Plant (cut or soft land into overhead). Cost (dive in). Door (land back). That is the full nightly grammar. More is a demo reel.

### Scene-change rule

A scene change that *earns* a blend is a new time **or** a new Double **or** a new place **between a-has**. Even then, the group daily should usually have **one** a-ha. A second 2D bridge is legal only if a second Double is load-bearing (a relationship, not a cameo). A third a-ha is almost always a recap. Cut it.

Inside an a-ha (new angle, they walk): hard cut, stay cinematic.

## 6. Voiceover (what to say, what never to say)

Spoken spine, same five moves, group and personal:

1. Want — named person, what they wanted tonight.
2. Pressure — who or what stood in the way.
3. Turn — the unexpected beat, smallest true sentence. Do not say the word “turn.”
4. Cost — what it took. Dignity. No pile-on.
5. Honest open question — unfinished, tomorrow.

Door is a look. Timestamps are written. Voiceover never points (“go see this talk,” “this is the same person in 2D,” “watch the pixels”).

Clips are silent under voiceover: the ear stays human. Picture maps. Mouth does not.

Do not pad the Cost sentence to cover the dive. Do not spend breaths teaching sprites. Day-in-life is not a longer voiceover budget.

## 8. Production do / don’t

**Do**

- Cut the a-ha in cinematic. That is what people enjoy.
- Hit plant, one Cost dive, Door 2D, every group daily, same order.
- Match on the action (the leave, the look), not on the surface (pixels for their own sake).
- Keep names readable in every 2D flash.
- Fact-lock every spoken Turn and Cost to a timestamp.
- Ship Tuesday from the auto pipeline. Human polish is for a new type, not for each night.
- Keep dignity on boots. The turn can cost them. The cut is not a roast.

**Don’t**

- Don’t sit in 2D. Flash it.
- Don’t blend every cut.
- Don’t pixel-expand every transition.
- Don’t make a fully movie trailer with zero 2D in the moving picture.
- Don’t treat a still on the timestamp card as the 2D quota. The card does not teach seeing.
- Don’t invent coverage the ledger did not produce.
- Don’t use pretty rooms of a world people cannot enter.
- Don’t put product UI in the middle of the tape.
- Don’t dual-call-to-action (watch + create). Create is not the product yet.
- Don’t design as if 3D live already exists.
- Don’t explain the blend in voiceover.
