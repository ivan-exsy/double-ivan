#### Core 2D ↔ Cinematic Visual System

The shift between Phaser and cinematic reality must be treated as the central storytelling language—not as an occasional special effect.

##### 1\. Exact scene continuity

Every Phaser-to-cinematic transition must preserve:

* character position;  
* body orientation;  
* camera angle;  
* environmental layout;  
* time of day;  
* action already in progress;  
* emotional state.

The viewer should immediately understand that these are not two different scenes. They are two ways of seeing the same event.

##### 2\. Phaser shows the system; cinema reveals the experience

**Phaser 2D communicates:**

* that the world is running;  
* that multiple Doubles are acting autonomously;  
* that this is something the viewer can observe;  
* that choices can branch and be replayed.

**Cinematic 3D communicates:**

* the meaning behind a pause;  
* the tension in eye contact;  
* the emotional cost of a choice;  
* what the pixelated scene feels like to the people inside it.

The intended mental effect is:

“I know I am looking at a simulation, but I am watching a real relationship.”

##### 3\. Transition methods

Use several related transition types so the visual language feels intentional:

* **Camera dive:** push into a Phaser sprite until individual pixels expand and resolve into realistic skin, fabric, and light.  
* **Action match:** begin an action in 2D and complete it in cinematic 3D.  
* **Reflection transition:** show the realistic person inside a window, eye, puddle, or device reflection while the surrounding world remains pixelated.  
* **Pixel fracture:** allow a cinematic scene to break into blocks and return to Phaser when the viewer changes the approach.  
* **Triple alignment:** briefly overlap the Phaser Double, cinematic Double, and real person during the final act of courage.

Avoid arbitrary glitch transitions. Every transformation should reveal a deeper emotional layer or return the viewer to the observable simulation.

#### Transparent Device Rules

The device remains an important visual icon, but it must follow strict rules:

* It controls only the user’s or Double’s own behavior.  
* It never changes another person’s feelings.  
* It never guarantees success.  
* It must not include a **RELATIONSHIP SUCCESS** slider.  
* **FEAR** should not simply be switched off. Courage matters precisely because fear is still present.  
* The most important controls are **COURAGE**, **HONESTY**, and **KINDNESS**.  
* The device can appear in the opening fantasy and later return as the visual representation of how the viewer guides or commissions the Double.  
* The action that begins a rehearsal is always **DOUBLE IT\!**

#### Emotional Resolution

The final success is not that Katya says yes.

The final success is that the hero does.

This makes the closing line carry two meanings:

* the Double is the version of him that said yes to the frightening moment;  
* after watching that version, the real person says yes to becoming braver.

#### Sound Direction

* Begin with realistic café ambience and a restrained heartbeat.  
* Each self-control adjustment should produce a soft physical sound—glass resonance, breath, or tension—not a game-interface click.  
* When **DOUBLE IT\!** is pressed, blend the heartbeat into the rhythmic sound of the Phaser world activating.  
* Phaser scenes may contain simplified environmental audio, but the cinematic transformation should open into full spatial sound: chairs shifting, breath, fabric movement, room tone.  
* Use the same short musical phrase in three forms: synthetic in Phaser, orchestral or acoustic in cinematic 3D, and naturalistic in the real-world ending.  
* Remove nearly all music before the real hero says **“Hey.”**  
* Resolve the music only after the line **“The version of you that said yes.”**

## Hero Video SOT Playbook — Consolidated Reference

This section elevates the document to single source of truth status by preserving the finalized **Concept 1.3 — Eng (gpt)** scenario in full while integrating high-value principles borrowed from the earlier `video_playbook.md` (v1 Sim-Opening Trailer playbook, §4). No prior content was removed or altered.

### Purpose (adapted from playbook §4.1)

The hero video's primary job is to make every cold viewer feel like the lead character of their own story and convert passive curiosity ("I want to know what happens to them") into active desire ("I want to build my Doubland and invite my friends").

It answers five questions without becoming an explainer video:

1. What is a Double and why would I want one?  
2. What can I rehearse or discover about myself?  
3. How does the 2D simulation become real emotional experience?  
4. What happens when I press "DOUBLE IT\!"?  
5. Why start now?

### Terminology for Marketing Assets (trailer exception from playbook v2.1)

Approved override for hero, landing, and trailer contexts: "An AI version of you", "AI Doubles of real people", "AI twin of your personality" are permitted for first-touch clarity. Must immediately resolve into approved brand voice ("your Double", "personality twin", "the version of you that...").

### Voice & Tone (from playbook §3 \+ Appendix B)

Warm, omniscient narrator — Attenborough's measured cadence \+ Jim Dale's playful curiosity \+ Morgan Freeman's gravitas. Slightly amused observer who reveals social truth without mockery. Never mocks the Doubles.

### Emotion Sequence for Hero Video (adapted from six-emotion beat sheet)
