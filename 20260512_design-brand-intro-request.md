## 1. What we're commissioning, in one line

A **5–8 second branded opening** that plays first in every Doubland trailer — locked once, reused forever across all cohorts and seasons. Three components: **logo splash · iconic flyover · brand sound**. After this opening, the trailer cuts into the cohort-specific cold open ("They're family. In Doubland, their Doubles have to survive each other.").

This is the **first thing every first-time viewer sees**. Its job is to imprint "DOUBLAND" in memory so they recognize the brand on day-2's daily-recap trailer, on tomorrow's social share, on the third recommendation a friend sends them.

---

## 2. What Doubland is (90 seconds)

Doubland is an **AI-driven reality simulation**. Small groups of real people (4–15 per cohort) have their "Doubles" — LLM-powered autonomous agents — live inside a persistent simulated village. The Doubles eat, sleep, scheme, form alliances, betray each other, and vote one another out every night. The simulation runs 24/7; viewers watch live or scrub back through the timeline at doubland.ai.

The current season ("Who will stay alive") is a **survival format**: last Double standing wins. Each night ends with a council vote.

**Format DNA we need the brand to convey:**

1. **Real people, AI representations.** It's not fully fictional, not fully real — it's the version of a person you'd get if their social instincts had no off-switch. That's the dramatic promise.
2. **Persistent and unscripted.** No writer's room. The village runs continuously.
3. **Cinematic but intimate.** Reality TV competition format, but with a documentary-confessional emotional register (per Round 1+2 Reality TV consultation — see `20260511_realityTV-expert-request.md` for context).
4. **Slightly conspiratorial.** Like you weren't supposed to overhear it.

What it is NOT:
- Not slick / corporate (no SaaS-startup feel)
- Not horror / urgent (no red typography, no "shocking twist")
- Not game-show / brassy (no neon, no Wheel-of-Fortune vibes)
- Not Pixar / cute (no rounded sans-serifs, no playful primary colors)

The end-card variant 2 (the one with cream serif typography over the council platform shot) is **the reference aesthetic**. Match its palette and emotional register exactly.

## 3. The three deliverables

### Deliverable A — Wordmark / logo

**What:** the primary brand mark for "Doubland".

**Asset:**
- SVG (primary)
- Transparent PNG at 4K (3840×2160) and 1K (1280×720)
- Three color variants: cream-on-dark (primary, for trailers), dark-on-cream (inverse, for print/web headers), monochrome (single-color usage)

**Direction:**
- Serif preferred (matches end-card-2's editorial typography). Hand-cut feel preferred over a stock font.
- Could include a small symbol/glyph alongside the wordmark (e.g. the small "DC" monogram in end-card variant 1 hints at one) — your call whether glyph adds value or is noise.
- The two D's in DOUBLAND can be a typographic moment — but don't force a gimmick.
- Color: cream/off-white `#F2EBD8` primary; warm gold accent `#C8A86B` (the color we use elsewhere) optional.

**Reference shows that get this right** (for tone, not direct copy):
- The Traitors UK opening title — confident, cinematic, slightly intimate
- HBO Max series identity — restrained editorial typography
- Apple TV+ launch idents — clean, modern, with weight

**Reference shows we want to avoid:**
- Survivor 1.0-era logo (too "extreme outdoors")
- Big Brother logos (too game-show)
- Tech startup logo aesthetics in general

### Deliverable B — Brand splash card (the static frame)

**What:** a 1280×720 still frame featuring the wordmark on a brand-appropriate background, ready to use as the opening 2–3 seconds of every trailer.

**Asset:**
- PNG (transparent layers preferred if delivered as PSD/AE source; otherwise flat PNG)
- Final motion treatment is up to the design team — see "motion direction" below

**Visual structure:**
- **Background:** dark, cinematic, atmospheric. Variant 2's council platform shot is a candidate, but a separate brand-specific background (e.g. wide village pan, night sky over the_ville, a single doorway with light spilling out) may be stronger as a brand mark.
- **Wordmark placement:** centered horizontally; centered or slightly above center vertically.
- **Locked tagline (H2):** `What if?` — sits below the DOUBLAND wordmark. Two-line lockup. Cleared by IP counsel 2026-05-12 (non-blocking risk vs. Marvel/Disney's *"What If…?"* mark). Typography for the H2 should be visually distinct from H1 — smaller weight, possibly italic-serif or contrasting weight, in light gray `#CCCCCC` or muted cream. The composite mark `DOUBLAND — What if?` is the brand lockup that goes on every trailer, doubland.ai homepage, and future marketing.

**Motion direction (your call):**
- 0.0–0.5s: fade in from black
- 0.5–2.5s: wordmark holds; subtle ambient motion in background (light flicker, fog drift)
- 2.5–3.0s: hold; brand sound lands
- 3.0s: crossfade into the iconic flyover (Deliverable C)

**Total screen time: 3 seconds.**

### Deliverable C — Iconic establishing flyover

**What:** a 3–5 second cinematic shot of the_ville — the signature visual that plays right after the splash card, the same in every trailer.

**Asset:**
- 1280×720 MP4 (h264, 25 fps), or 4K source we can downscale
- Soundless (the audio bed is our brand sound + anthem)

**Visual direction:**
- A slow zoom-out OR slow pan from an iconic angle of the_ville.
- The_ville is a small fictional village — wooden cottages, a central council platform with chairs in a semi-circle (see `end_card_background.png`), string lights + lanterns, evergreen forest backdrop, fog/night atmosphere.
- Time-of-day: **night or dusk** preferred (matches the council-platform reference). The mystery + intimacy is harder to convey at daylight.
- Camera movement should feel **deliberate, not kinetic** — this is a brand-establishing shot, not an action beat.

**We already have 5 commissioned cinematic flyovers** in `video/fly-over/`. They were generated via Grok Imagine and are used in the trailer's stakes-montage section:
- `cinematic_flyover_village_overhead.mp4`
- `cinematic_flyover_homes_row_approach.mp4`
- `cinematic_flyover_hobbs_cafe_interior.mp4`
- `cinematic_flyover_cafe_exterior_pan.mp4`
- `cinematic_flyover_village_dusk_wind_down.mp4`

**Question for the design team:** is one of those five strong enough to serve as the brand flyover (with a possible re-cut/color-grade), or does the brand flyover need to be a purpose-shot commission distinct from the stakes-montage flyovers? Reviewing the five and giving us your read is part of the deliverable.

**Total screen time: 4–5 seconds.**

### Deliverable D — Brand sound (optional, can defer)

**What:** a 1–2 second audio cue that plays under/at the end of the brand splash card. Think Apple TV+ note, Netflix "ta-dum", HBO static.

**Asset:**
- 1× WAV (48 kHz stereo, normalized −14 LUFS)
- 1× MP3 192 kbps export

**Direction:**
- Single sustained note OR 2-note motif — not a long phrase.
- Texture: cinematic / orchestral, warm. Could include a soft anthem string note + a single bell or chime.
- **NOT** game-show sting, **NOT** corporate brand "ding", **NOT** percussive.

**If deferring:** the anthem track's opening note (already in `video/audio/music_anthem.mp3`) can carry the audio bed of the splash card silently. Document the deferral in the deliverable spec.

## 4. Where these assets land in the trailer

Total brand-opening duration: **5–8 seconds**, slotted before the existing cold open.

| Time | Element | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–0.5s | Fade in from black + brand sound onset | Deliverable D (or anthem opening note) |
| 0.5–3.0s | Brand splash card with wordmark | Deliverable B |
| 3.0–3.5s | Crossfade to iconic flyover | (transition) |
| 3.5–8.0s | Iconic flyover — slow zoom out over the_ville | Deliverable C |
| 8.0–8.5s | Crossfade into cold open | (transition) |
| 8.5s onward | Existing cold open contradiction: "They're family. In Doubland, their Doubles have to survive each other." | Existing trailer |

This pushes the rest of the trailer back by ~5–8 seconds. Total runtime becomes ~135–138s (from the current 124–130s). Acceptable; the daily-recap trailer cadence has the same opening so viewers get the brand familiarity bonus across every video.

## 5. Brand palette + typography (already established)

Match the end-card-2 aesthetic:

- **Primary cream:** `#F2EBD8` (wordmark, large text)
- **Secondary light gray:** `#CCCCCC` (supporting text)
- **Warm gold accent:** `#C8A86B` (underlines, dividers, key highlights — sparingly)

**Constraints:**
- All assets must work at 1280×720 (master) and crop reasonably to 1080×1920 9:16 (we're shipping 16:9-only in v2.1 but will revisit 9:16 in v2.x).
- All assets must be ours to use commercially in perpetuity. No stock-photo or stock-music dependencies without explicit licensing.
- Filenames and folder structure: deliver in a single zipped folder; the engineering team will move them into `video/assets/production/brand/`.
- One round of feedback expected; second round on request.

**Open questions where we want your view:**

1. **Wordmark glyph or not?** Is "DOUBLAND" strong as a pure wordmark, or does it need a small accompanying symbol (like the DC monogram hinted at in variant 1)?

2. **Which of the 5 existing flyovers (if any) can serve as the brand flyover?** Or commission new?

3. **Tagline below the wordmark — yes/no?** If yes, what 3–4 words?

4. **Brand sound — commission now, or rely on the anthem's opening note?** If commissioning, what reference cues should we point you at?

5. **Splash-card background — separate atmospheric shot, or layered over the iconic flyover's first frame?** The latter is structurally simpler; the former gives the wordmark its own visual space.

---

## 7. Success criteria

The brand opening succeeds when:
1. A first-time viewer can name the brand ("Doubland") after watching the trailer once.
2. A returning viewer (day 2 daily-recap) recognizes the brand identity from the opening 3 seconds and knows what franchise they're watching.
3. The opening feels like a brand statement, not a logo-stuck-on-existing-footage afterthought.
4. The brand visual identity is reusable for: trailer openings, social media posts, doubland.ai homepage, future cohort marketing, eventual broadcast/streaming distribution.
