## 4. The seven questions

Please respond as briefly as possible — terse, prescriptive answers are more useful than long ones at this stage.

### Closing structure

1. **One card or two for a competition-format opener?** Most franchises seem to end with a single end card + maybe a brief sponsor card. Are we overcomplicating with "how-to-watch" *and* "end card" as separate beats? Should we merge to one 14–18 s closing card?

2. **What's the single most important information on the final on-screen frame?** Trying to pick between: (a) the cohort/season question ("Pistsov Family — Who will stay alive?"), (b) the URL (doubland.ai), (c) the daily cadence ("New trailer at 6:30 PM"), (d) the start-now beat ("Day 1 starts now"). If forced to keep ONE on the final frame, which earns its place?

3. **Hierarchy across the cards.** If we keep two cards, what's the right division of labor? Our instinct: card 1 = format (24/7 + what to watch + URL); card 2 = cohort-specific cliffhanger (cohort name + start-now + recap cadence). Right? Wrong? Different split?

### Narration tail

4. **Narrate through to the end, or let it close on music?** Our narrator stops at 1:52. The remaining 18 s plays anthem-only over the cards. Is that the right call, or should narration carry through, e.g. one more line over the cards: *"This is Doubland. Tonight, the village opens."*?

5. **If we narrate through, where does the energy peak land?** The current habit hook is the last spoken line. If we add a closing narration over the cards, does that dilute the habit hook, or does it elevate the close?

### Copy specifics on our redraft

6. **Does the line "Watch: Surprises. Drama. Insights." earn its place**, or is it generic Wikipedia-list-of-tropes copy (you flagged a similar issue in Round 2 with our earlier cycling phrases)? If generic, what's a better treatment?

7. **"Who will stay alive?" as a question on the end card** — does ending on a question read as confident/curious or as overwrought? Reality TV opens often end on confident statements ("39 days, 18 survivors..."), not questions. Are we breaking a useful convention here, or is the question form right for our format?
