## 3. Owner craft notes (non-negotiable for the rewrite)

### 3.1 Glue the person into one story — don’t fragment them

**Problem in the auto draft:** the same Double is re-introduced in isolated pieces with repetitive job/place lines, e.g.:

- *Ivan is a pharmacy technician at The Willows… Truth-seeking…*  
- *Ivan Pitts, a pharmacy technician at The Willows…, scans the room…*  
- *Ivan's plan: use the challenge as a data-gathering exercise…*

That burns runtime and forces the listener to glue fragments.

**Rule:** For each featured Double, **stamp once** (who they are + what they want tonight), then **continue the story** with first names. Re-state job/place only if a beat truly needs it for clarity — never as a refrain.

**Goal:** Tell more with fewer words. One cohesive throughline beats three caption cards.

### 3.2 Events can be their own section; named Doubles may reappear

When narrating a dedicated event (e.g. today’s challenge → vote), it is fine to have that as a **clear section** of the VO. Re-mentioning already-introduced Doubles is good when it **serves the plot** (who was safe, who was exposed, who moved).

Do **not** re-bio them. Do **not** restart their stamp.

### 3.3 Simple, descriptive narrator — do the glue for the listener

- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs a 12-year-old pictures in one listen.  
- No strategy jargon (*behavioral map, data alliance, fractured map, data-gathering exercise*) unless immediately translated into plain stakes.  
- No bullet-point cadence. Sentences should **cause** the next sentence.  
- Narrator’s job: make the plot **easy**. Listener should not have to assemble isolated lines into a story.

**Rule of thumb:** if a sharp 12-year-old wouldn’t get it on one listen with sound only, rewrite.
