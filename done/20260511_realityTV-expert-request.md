## 1. What Doubland is, in 90 seconds

Doubland is an **AI-driven reality simulation** where small groups of real people are represented by autonomous "doubles" — LLM-powered agents that act on each person's behalf inside a persistent simulated village. The doubles eat, sleep, gossip, form alliances, scheme, betray, and vote each other out, 24 hours a day, in real time. Their decisions are unscripted and emerge from the LLM's reasoning over each persona's profile, memories, and current situation.

**Three things that matter for the format:**

1. **Real people in the cast.** Each cohort is 4–15 real humans who've agreed to have their double play. The audience knows these are real people's "AI doppelgängers." This creates a personal stake unmatched by fictional characters or even other reality TV — viewers are watching what their friend's AI self would do under social pressure.

2. **Survival mode.** Doubles compete in elimination rounds. Each simulated day ends with a vote; one persona leaves. The last double standing "survives" the season. (Working title for the current season: "Who will stay alive.")

3. **Persistent, unscripted, viewable live.** The simulation runs continuously on Doubland's servers. Viewers can drop in any time at doubland.ai, scrub back through the timeline, follow specific doubles, and watch interactions first-hand. Every conversation is fully traceable and unscripted.

**The asset we want to perfect:** the **opener trailer** — a ~130-second video that introduces a new cohort to the audience and converts first-time viewers into daily-recap watchers. The first cohort being launched is the "Pistsov family" — four members of a single family whose doubles are competing.

## 5. Where we want to go — proposed new 5-beat structure

We're considering restructuring the trailer to follow a deeper narrative arc:

1. **Standard cold open** — introduce the *concept* (what is this thing?), the *challenge* (what's at stake?), the *expectations* (what kind of show is this?). ~10–15 s.

2. **Cast of characters** — introduce each double, but framed by **what makes them dangerous, lovable, or unpredictable** in a survival format. Why should the viewer care about *this specific person*? ~60–80 s for 4 cast members.

3. **The major drama of the day** — *who* clashed with *why* and *how*. Tease the most explosive moment from the cohort's first simulated day. ~15–20 s.

4. **Outcome → lesson learned** — what happened, what it revealed about the cast, what's now different. The "previously on…" but for the season premiere. ~10–15 s.

5. **Sneak peek to the future** — what's coming, who's about to crack, who's positioning for power. CTA: watch live at doubland.ai. ~10–15 s.

We have **no commitment to this exact structure** — it's a strawman. If your experience tells you this is wrong, please push back hard.

## 6. The audience funnel we're trying to build

This is the success metric. Every line of narration, every visual, every music choice should be evaluated against this funnel:

1. **First-time view** — viewer stumbles on the trailer (social, link, embed). Hooks them in 5 seconds; holds them for 130 s.
2. **Daily habit** — viewer returns at 6:30 PM the next day to watch the day-overview trailer. Same the next day. And the next.
3. **Live obsession** — viewer starts dropping into doubland.ai mid-day to watch raw simulation moments first-hand. Picks favorites. Argues with friends about who'll get voted out.
4. **Viewer evangelist** — viewer shares trailers, screenshots, "did you see what Gosha just did" moments. Pulls friends in.
5. **Cohort member** — viewer applies (or pays) to put their own double in a future cohort. Brings their friend group.

The opener trailer is funnel-step 1. Its job is to make funnel-step 2 inevitable.
