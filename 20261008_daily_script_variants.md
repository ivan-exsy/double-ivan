# Inquiry — which daily trailer script should we proceed with?

**Date:** 2026-10-08
**To:** scenario writer
**From:** Doubland
**Ask:** Read the night, then the three scripts. Tell us which direction to build. You may pick one script as it stands, change the rules of one of the three writers, or propose a new writer.

This is a decision about the **spoken story** only. Pictures, voice recording, and publishing come after the words are chosen. Please do not rewrite the scripts into a finished trailer. Tell us which shape to proceed with, and why.

---

## What we are making

Doubland runs a simulated day. Each person is a Double: an AI version of a real adult. They live, talk, work, and, on a Survival night, play a social game. Nobody writes their dialogue in advance.

The daily trailer is a vertical short for a cold viewer. They do not know the people or the game. After one watch they should be able to:

- say what a Double is
- name the two people the night turned on
- know that someone won protection and someone went home
- feel one real choice
- want to see a named person who is still in tomorrow

It is the story of **one night**, not a tour of the whole day. A full recap of every conversation is the wrong product.

Length follows the night. About a minute is normal. Padding to hit a clock is a failure. So is a script with no real choice.

The words must come from the night’s record. The writer may not invent a motive, a second person leaving, a challenge rule, or which step broke a tie. A recorded reason may be spoken with the speaker’s name. A feeling the record does not contain should stay unsaid.

The first Survival night also has to teach, once: these are Doubles; someone is voted out every night until one remains; alliances become votes; everyone casts a ballot. Later nights drop that lesson. This night is a first Survival night, so the lesson is in bounds.

## Answers before a fourth writer

These are the limits a fourth playbook has to live inside. Same night, same five people.

### 1. What the writer actually gets

Three layers. They are not the same pile.

**What the sim recorded.** Public talks sit on the movement log as the conversation grew, with a step we can turn into a clock time. Each Double also has memory rows (things that happened, and thoughts). The challenge stores each person’s hold or fold and the reason they gave. The vote stores who voted for whom, a reason, and Yevgenia’s farewell.

**What this night’s packet kept.** 21 unique public talks. Each one is already cut to **6 turns**, and each turn is cut at **25 words** (a longer turn ends in …). Two of the 21 were longer than 6 turns and are marked truncated. Every talk has a step and a clock time. On top of that, each person contributes at most **5 moments**: up to 2 of those talks, up to 2 thoughts, and the rest events. This night’s packet has 10 thoughts, 9 chat snippets, and 5 events across the five people.

**What the model was shown.** A talk reaches the model as a **summary of the first 3 turns** of that already-cut transcript, plus a short headline. The model does not see turn 4, 5, or 6. It does not see the clock. A two-line exchange can be used verbatim only when both lines survived that cut and landed in those first 3 turns.

The uncut talk still exists in the sim. Handing the writer the 6-turn, 25-word transcript is a packet change, not a new simulation. Handing them the uncut conversation is a larger collect change. Until one of those ships, do not write a playbook that depends on a full verbatim scene.

### 2. Memory and reflection notes

Yes, each Double has thought rows in memory. They are not a diary, and they are not the private chat between a person and their Double. Those private chats stay out.

The trailer packet keeps two thoughts per person, ranked by how sharp the memory system marked them, not by a fresh search for “what matters to tonight’s vote.”

On this night those ten thoughts are not usable inner life. They are leftover greetings copied into memory, or stale game notes: “Luba left tonight,” “Elfi left tonight,” “I won the Alliance Lock-In.” None of that is what happened. A line like “Elfi wrote that…” would be inventing a feeling, or repeating a false note.

The inner lines that are safe to speak are the ones stored on a **decision**, with the speaker’s name:

- Challenge: why each person held or folded (already in the night section).
- Vote: why each person voted as they did, including promises they say they kept or broke (next answer).

If a thought is not a real reflection of this night, leave it out. Do not paraphrase it into a mood.

### 3. Is there a drama signal?

Yes. It is already in the vote record. The current writers do not receive it as “this is the story,” which is why they fall back to the challenge and the cafe tour.

For each voter the record has: who they voted for, a reason, a confidence from 0.3 to 0.7, and a list of promises in the other person’s words marked **keeping** or **breaking**.

The sharp pair is mutual. Yevgenia promised Elfi, “I meant what I said earlier — I’ll vote with you,” and the record says she is breaking that. She voted for Elfi. Elfi promised Yevgenia they would watch each other’s backs through the vote, and the record says Elfi is breaking that. Elfi voted for Luba. Ivan and Marsha voted for Yevgenia. Luba voted for Elfi. That is the 2–2 tie.

Elfi’s reason and Luba’s reason both open with “No strong preference” and sit at confidence 0.3. Do not build the night on those two essays. Yevgenia is 0.6, Ivan 0.7, Marsha 0.65. Even there, speak the promise and the vote. Do not add a motive the sentence does not contain.

There is no separate flag for “first meeting,” “public disagreement,” or “broken plan.” Alliance breaks exist as rows, and they do not say who ended them. Keyword hits (vote, alliance, betray) only decide which talks get into the short pile. They are not a story choice.

A fourth writer can be told: look first for a promise the same person broke with their ballot. If that pair is missing or low-confidence, fall back to the challenge reason and the farewell. Do not start from the Shield just because it is the loudest label.

One speech rule stays: this was a tie, then a tiebreak. Do not say “2 votes land on Yevgenia.” The safe spoken line is the tie, then that she is gone. Do not invent which step broke the tie.

### 4. Footage away from the challenge

Every scene in the day is already on screen in the live game (Phaser). A cafe talk is as picturable as the challenge. The script stage does not pick pictures, but the picture path does not depend on a camera crew.

For a beat we choose, production does two things:

1. Capture a few seconds of the Phaser scene at that step, so the viewer sees the live game: who is in the room, where they stand.
2. Morph that into a cinematic rendering with Grok Imagine. The inputs are the interior or exterior photo of that place, the photos of the Doubles in the scene, and the description of the scene from the logs.

Pittsburgh place photos are on disk, including the PPG Cafe interior. They are a reference for Imagine, not the shot the viewer sits on. An older Hold-for-the-Shield clip from a different cast is the wrong picture for this night. This night gets its own capture and its own rendering.

What that means for the fourth writer:

- You may open on the cafe, the vote, or the challenge. Lack of footage is not a reason to prefer the Shield.
- The spoken scene and the log description should be the same moment. Imagine is given that description. A line we invent cannot be pictured honestly.
- The capture needs a step where those people are on the map. The 21 public talks have a step and a place. A thought with no place cannot be a scene.
- The trailer shows a few seconds of the map, then the cinematic of that same moment. Write the beat so both can carry it. Do not write a shot that only works as a movie and never as the live game.

### 5. Who the trailer is for

Both, in this order.

The product at launch is invite-only, adults, friend groups. The first people who care are the real adults behind the Doubles, and their friends. Small true details (the daughter’s dinner, the lumber lists, the farewell about houses) are for them.

The closer is still written so a **stranger** can follow it. A shared clip may be someone’s first look, on a night that is not episode 1. After one watch a stranger should name the two people, know someone won protection and someone went home, feel one real choice, and want tomorrow for a named person who is still in.

That is why the current order is stakes, then the person. Friends still get the personal line, after they know what the night cost. We have not tested the reverse (personal detail first, stakes later). If you want that order, say so as a rule for the fourth writer, and keep the stranger test: they must still know who left.

### 6. Watch data

None. These three scripts were never published. There is no retention curve, no drop-off second, and no view count to write toward. Older closers were founder watches of a different cast. Do not infer a length or a beat order from audience data. We do not have any.

### 7. Fact-lock and consent

Fact-lock stays on. A person consenting to a Double is not a step where they approve a trailer line, and we do not have that step.

Spoken inner life has to be a recorded decision reason or a recorded thought that is actually about this night, with the speaker named. A mood the record does not contain stays unsaid, including for someone who would probably nod at it. Private chats between a person and their Double stay out of the trailer.

If a line is cruel, or it quotes a stale memory as if it happened tonight, cut it. Consent is not a waiver.
