## 1. Pipeline Overview

```
Simulation day completes
  │
  ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│  STEP 1: Data Extraction        │  Query Supabase for full day log
│  (automated, ~5 sec)            │  Actions, convos, thoughts, locations, plans
└──────────────┬──────────────────┘
               │
               ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│  STEP 2: Showrunner LLM Call    │  Single prompt → structured trailer script
│  (automated, ~30-60 sec)        │  Picks story, writes narration, specifies shots
└──────────────┬──────────────────┘
               │
               ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│  STEP 3: Audio Production       │  TTS renders narrator VO
│  (automated, ~30 sec)           │  Select mood track, trim to 60s
└──────────────┬──────────────────┘
               │
               ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│  STEP 4: Video Capture          │  Screen-record Phaser playback (MVP: semi-manual)
│  (semi-manual, ~15-20 min)      │  Follow shot list, operate camera
└──────────────┬──────────────────┘
               │
               ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│  STEP 5: Compositing            │  FFmpeg: video + VO + music + subtitles → MP4
│  (automated, ~2 min)            │  60s final cut, 9:16 crop for social
└─────────────────────────────────┘
```

**MVP time budget: ~25 minutes per trailer** (Steps 1-3 automated, Step 4 is the manual bottleneck).

## 2. Storytelling Weight Model

Your visual medium is top-down 2D sprites on a tile map — no faces, no body language, limited animation. The storytelling load shifts dramatically toward audio and text.

| Channel | Weight | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Narration (VO) | 35% | Carries emotional arc, reveals motives, creates dramatic irony |
| Text overlays / subtitles / cards | 20% | Contextualizes action, surfaces dialogue, creates rhythm |
| Camera work (zoom, pan, speed) | 25% | Creates focus, pacing, scene structure, spatial storytelling |
| Music + sound design | 15% | Sets mood, marks transitions, builds tension and release |
| Sprite movement (visual) | 5% | Provides spatial grounding — "proof" that it happened |

**Core principle:** The sprites are *evidence*. The narrator is the *storyteller*. The viewer should feel like they're watching surveillance footage narrated by someone who knows everything.

## 3. Data Extraction

After a simulation day completes, query the following from Supabase. All queries target a single `simulation_id` and a single `persona_id` (the protagonist).

### 3a. Primary data (feeds the showrunner LLM)

| Data | Source | Query | Key fields |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Day's memories** | `dbl_memory` | `dbl_get_sim_memories(agent_id, sim_id, ['event','thought','chat'], limit=500)` | `memory_type`, `content`, `poignancy`, `subject/predicate/object`, `keywords[]` |
| **Position history** | `personas_coords` | `get_all_step_positions(sim_name, from_step, to_step)` | `step`, `x`, `y`, `description` |
| **Scratch state** | `persona_scratch` | `load_persona_scratch(sim_name, persona_name)` | `daily_req`, `f_daily_schedule`, `relationship_affinity`, `learned` |
| **Chat transcripts** | `dbl_memory` (type=chat) | Filter memories where `memory_type='chat'` | `content` (summary), node `filling` (full transcript) |
| **Reflections** | `dbl_memory` (type=thought) | Filter memories where `memory_type='thought'` | `content`, `poignancy`, `keywords` |

### 3b. Supporting data (for spatial context and other characters)

| Data | Source | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **All personas' positions** | `get_all_step_positions` (all personas) | Identify proximity events, co-location |
| **Other personas' actions** | `personas_coords.description` | Context for what others were doing during interactions |
| **Proximity events** | Step JSON `proximities[]` | Detect who was near whom and when |

## 4. Showrunner LLM — Prompt & Output

A single LLM call receives the full `day_log` and the playbook rules, then outputs a complete trailer script.

### System prompt

```
You are the showrunner for "Double" — a Truman Show-style reality series about
AI personas living in a virtual village. You create 60-second Day-in-Life trailers
from simulation data.

Your trailers feel like watching surveillance footage narrated by a warm, omniscient
voice — part nature documentary, part Truman Show. You find the human drama in
mundane daily life. You notice what characters don't notice about themselves.

RULES:
- Exactly 60 seconds. 5 scenes maximum.
- One protagonist. Their name is always spoken first in the trailer.
- Omniscient third-person narration. Never address the viewer directly.
- Find the single most interesting thread of the day and build around it.
- Create dramatic irony: reveal what the protagonist doesn't know.
- The hook must be the most surprising or emotionally loaded moment — shown first,
  explained later.
- Never resolve the story fully. End on an open question or quiet revelation.
- Narration word count: 120-150 words (paced at ~2.2 words/sec with pauses).
- Use silence strategically — at least one 2-3 second pause for a visual beat.
- Specify camera directions in terms of: zoom level (0.5-2.0), follow target,
  pan direction, playback speed (1x, 2x, 5x, 10x, 25x).
- Choose one mood: intrigue, drama, or wholesome.
```

### User prompt template

```
Here is the full day log for {protagonist_name}, Day {sim_day}:

<day_log>
{day_log_json}
</day_log>

Create a 60-second Day-in-Life trailer. Output valid JSON matching this schema:

{
  "title": "Day {N} — {evocative subtitle}",
  "mood": "intrigue" | "drama" | "wholesome",
  "protagonist": "{name}",
  "logline": "One sentence that captures the day's dramatic core",
  "narrator_script": "Full narration text with [PAUSE 2s] markers and [SCENE N] markers",
  "scenes": [
    {
      "scene_id": 1,
      "label": "hook" | "setup" | "development" | "turn" | "close",
      "time_range_sec": [0, 8],
      "step_range": [start_step, end_step],
      "focus_persona": "name",
      "location": "human-readable location",
      "camera": {
        "start_zoom": 1.0,
        "end_zoom": 1.5,
        "follow": "persona_name" | null,
        "pan_to": {"x": tile_x, "y": tile_y} | null,
        "playback_speed": 1
      },
      "transition_in": "fade_from_black" | "scene_cut" | "fly_over" | "time_lapse" | "focus_shift" | "card_break",
      "transition_out": "scene_cut" | "fly_over" | "time_lapse" | "focus_shift" | "card_break" | "fade_to_black",
      "narrator_lines": ["Line 1.", "Line 2."],
      "subtitle_card": "2-5 word card text" | null,
      "key_dialogue": ["Speaker: line", ...] | null,
      "visual_note": "Brief description of what viewer should see"
    }
  ],
  "end_card": {
    "text": "DAY {N} — {VILLAGE_NAME}",
    "subtitle": "tagline or open question"
  }
}
```

### Model selection

Use **Tier C** (gpt-5.2 or equivalent high-reasoning model) for the showrunner call. Creative coherence matters more than cost here — this is one call per trailer.

## 5. 60-Second Beat Sheet

Every Day-in-Life trailer follows this rhythm. The showrunner LLM should produce scenes that map to these beats.

```
0:00 ─── HOOK (5-8s) ──────────────────────────────────────────
│  The most surprising or emotionally loaded moment of the day.
│  Shown without context. Narrator drops one intriguing line.
│  Camera: tight zoom (1.5-2.0x) on protagonist.
│  Transition in: fade from black.
│  Music: first note of mood track — sparse, atmospheric.
│
0:08 ─── SETUP (10-12s) ────────────────────────────────────────
│  Rewind to the morning. Establish routine and normalcy.
│  Narrator introduces protagonist and their day's plan.
│  Camera: bird's-eye (0.5-0.7x) → slowly zoom in.
│  Playback speed: 5-10x during walking/transit, 1x for key moments.
│  Subtitle card: protagonist name + one-line identity.
│  Music: rhythm enters (light percussion).
│
0:20 ─── DEVELOPMENT (15-18s) ──────────────────────────────────
│  The main thread unfolds. Key interaction or event.
│  Show the conversation or encounter that matters most.
│  Camera: medium zoom (1.0-1.2x), follow protagonist.
│  Pull 1-2 dialogue lines as text overlays.
│  Narrator reveals context the protagonist doesn't have.
│  Music: builds (add melodic elements).
│
0:38 ─── TURN (10-12s) ─────────────────────────────────────────
│  Something shifts. A realization, a contradiction, an arrival.
│  This is the moment that reframes the day.
│  Camera: pause or slow push-in. Consider a 2-3s silence.
│  Narrator delivers the most loaded line of the trailer.
│  Music: brief stop-down (drop to near-silence), then swell.
│
0:50 ─── CLOSE (8-10s) ─────────────────────────────────────────
│  Don't resolve. Show the aftermath or the question.
│  Protagonist walks away, sits alone, or continues unaware.
│  Camera: slow zoom out to bird's-eye.
│  Narrator: final line — open-ended, reflective.
│  Music: resolves to a single sustained note or fades.
│
0:58 ─── END CARD (2s) ─────────────────────────────────────────
│  "DAY 17 — THE VILLE"
│  Fade to black.
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

**Pacing rules:**
- 120-150 words of narration total (~2.2 words/sec with pauses)
- At least one silence of 2-3 seconds (let the visual breathe)
- Maximum 2 dialogue excerpts shown as text overlay
- Maximum 3 subtitle cards (including end card)
- Speed ramps only during transit/routine — never during emotional moments

## 6. Narrator Voice

### Tone

**Warm omniscient.** The narrator knows everything — what happened, what will happen, what the protagonist is thinking. But the tone is gentle, curious, slightly amused. Never mocking. Never cold. The narrator *likes* these people.

Think: a nature documentary narrator who studies human behavior with the same reverence David Attenborough brings to wildlife. Slightly old-fashioned cadence — unhurried, considered, with weight on key words.

### Specific voice qualities

| Quality | Target | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Pacing | Deliberate, ~2.0-2.5 words/sec | Rushed, breathless, podcast-speed |
| Register | Warm baritone or warm alto | Raspy, breathy, or nasal |
| Emotion | Genuine curiosity, quiet delight | Sarcastic, deadpan, over-enthusiastic |
| Age feel | 40-60 (experienced, trustworthy) | Young/casual or elderly/frail |
| Accent | Neutral/slight British warmth | Strong regional, exaggerated |

### Reference voices (in order of relevance)

See [Appendix B](#appendix-b-narrator-reference-voices) for detailed reference analysis. The primary references:

1. **David Attenborough** — the gold standard for observational warmth. His cadence treats every subject as worthy of wonder. This is the *tone* target.
2. **Jim Dale (Pushing Daisies narrator)** — omniscient, whimsical, storybook quality. Knows everything, reveals it with a twinkle. This is the *personality* target.
3. **Morgan Freeman (March of the Penguins)** — warm authority without condescension. This is the *trust* target.

The canonical Double voice should land in the intersection: **Attenborough's cadence + Jim Dale's omniscient playfulness + Freeman's warmth.**

### Narration writing rules

1. **Omniscient third person only.** "Katya had no idea..." not "Watch what happens..."
2. **Name the protagonist in the first line.** Always.
3. **Create dramatic irony.** Reveal what the character doesn't know: "What Katya didn't realize was that Gosha had been sitting in that cafe for two hours, waiting."
4. **One devastating observation per trailer.** The line that makes the viewer feel something. Save it for the turn (0:38-0:50).
5. **End on a question or unresolved image.** "And as the sun set on Day 17, Katya still hadn't opened the message." Not "Katya learned an important lesson."
6. **Short sentences for impact. Longer sentences for flow.** Mix them.
7. **[PAUSE 2s]** markers indicate where the narrator goes silent and the visual carries the moment.

### TTS setup (MVP)

**ElevenLabs** recommended for voice quality and consistency.

1. Go to ElevenLabs Voice Library
2. Search for voices matching: male, mature, warm, British-neutral, narrative
3. Shortlist 3-5 voices. Test each with this calibration line:
   > "Katya had planned a quiet afternoon at the library. But the library, it turned out, had other plans for Katya."
4. Select the voice that sounds most like a warm documentary narrator — not an audiobook reader, not a commercial VO.
5. Settings: Stability 0.65, Clarity 0.75, Style 0.40 (slightly expressive, not flat)

**Alternative:** OpenAI TTS `nova` or `onyx` voice with slow speed setting. Less distinctive but zero setup.

## 7. Music Strategy

Three pre-generated tracks — one per mood mode. Each track should be 75 seconds (gives 15s of editing headroom on a 60s trailer). Generated on **Suno** or **Udio**.

### Track specifications

| Track | Mood | BPM | Key elements | Energy arc |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Intrigue** | Curious, voyeuristic, Truman-esque | 80-95 | Sparse piano, light strings, subtle electronic pulse, soft clock-tick motif | Low → medium build → gentle resolve |
| **Drama** | Tense, emotional, stakes | 100-115 | Emotional strings, building percussion, piano bass notes, sub-bass swells | Low → steady build → dramatic swell → sudden drop → final hit |
| **Wholesome** | Warm, hopeful, gentle humor | 90-105 | Acoustic guitar, warm piano, soft pads, light glockenspiel/marimba | Medium warmth → gentle build → warm peak → soft landing |

### Track structure (mapped to beat sheet)

All three tracks should follow this energy curve to sync with the 60s beat sheet:

```
0:00-0:08  HOOK        │ ░░          │ Atmospheric. Single instrument. Space.
0:08-0:20  SETUP       │ ░░░░        │ Rhythm enters. Light percussion. Foundation.
0:20-0:38  DEVELOPMENT │ ░░░░░░░     │ Melody joins. Steady build. Layers add.
0:38-0:42  STOP-DOWN   │ ░           │ Near-silence. One sustained note or nothing.
0:42-0:50  TURN        │ ░░░░░░░░░   │ Swell. Emotional peak. Full arrangement.
0:50-0:58  CLOSE       │ ░░░░        │ Resolve. Strip back to single instrument.
0:58-0:60  END CARD    │ ░           │ Final note sustain or fade to silence.
```

See [Appendix A](#appendix-a-sunoudio-music-prompts) for exact generation prompts.

### Music editing rules

- **Never let music compete with narration.** Duck music 6-9 dB under VO.
- **Stop-down is sacred.** At the turn (~0:38-0:42), music must drop to near-silence. This is the most powerful moment in the trailer.
- **No lyrics.** Instrumental only. Vocals (wordless "ooh/aah") acceptable only in wholesome track.

## 9. Transition Library

Six transition types available for the showrunner to specify between scenes.

### T1: Scene Cut
**What:** Hard cut to a new camera position.
**When:** Between any scenes. The workhorse transition.
**Camera:** Instant reposition + zoom change. No animation.
**Audio:** Optional soft whoosh (SFX #1). Or nothing.
**Duration:** 0 frames (instant).

### T2: Fly-Over
**What:** Zoom out from current scene → bird's-eye pan across village → zoom in on new scene.
**When:** Major location change. Establishing spatial relationship between scenes.
**Camera:** Current zoom → 0.5x → pan to new location → zoom to target level.
**Audio:** Slow whoosh (SFX #2) during pan. Optional ambient village hum.
**Duration:** 3-4 seconds.
**Note:** This is the signature "Truman Show control room" transition. Use max once per trailer.

### T3: Time Lapse
**What:** Fast-forward playback to compress routine/transit time.
**When:** Between morning routine and first event. Between locations. Compressing uneventful hours.
**Camera:** Medium zoom (0.8-1.0x), follow protagonist. Playback at 10-25x speed.
**Audio:** Clock tick sequence (SFX #4) over music. Narrator may comment during lapse.
**Duration:** 3-6 seconds of real time (compressing 30min-2hr of sim time).
**Visual:** Consider subtle speed-line overlay or clock widget in corner.

### T4: Focus Shift
**What:** Camera stays in same area but shifts zoom/focus from one sprite to another.
**When:** Two characters in same location. Revealing who else is present.
**Camera:** Zoom/pan smoothly from sprite A to sprite B (1-2s tween).
**Audio:** None needed. Let narration carry it.
**Duration:** 1-2 seconds.

### T5: Card Break
**What:** Cut to a text card on solid/semi-transparent background.
**When:** Time jumps ("3 hours later..."), context setting ("Meanwhile, across the village..."), dramatic emphasis.
**Camera:** N/A (full-screen card).
**Audio:** Typing SFX (SFX #9) as text appears. Music continues underneath.
**Duration:** 1.5-2.5 seconds.
**Text style:** See [Section 13](#13-subtitle--card-style-guide).

### T6: Fade to Black
**What:** Gradual fade to black, hold, fade up on new scene.
**When:** Act breaks. Before/after the turn. Opening. Closing.
**Camera:** Current shot fades. New shot fades in.
**Audio:** Music may continue through (L-cut) or stop-down.
**Duration:** 1.5-2.5 seconds (0.5s fade out + 0.5-1s hold + 0.5s fade in).

### Transition selection guide

| From → To | Recommended transition |
|---|---|
| Hook → Setup | **Card break** ("That morning...") or **Fade to black** |
| Setup → Development | **Time lapse** (compress transit) or **Scene cut** |
| Development → Turn | **Focus shift** or **Scene cut** with silence drop |
| Turn → Close | **Fade to black** (with stop-down) |
| Close → End card | **Fade to black** |
| Any major location change | **Fly-over** (use once max) or **Scene cut** |

## 10. Video Assembly (MVP)

The MVP pipeline uses semi-manual screen recording since the frontend lacks a programmatic camera scripting API.

### Prerequisites

- OBS Studio (free) or similar screen recorder
- Phaser frontend running in browser at simulation playback URL
- The showrunner's JSON output (your shot list)
- Familiarity with playback controls: play/pause, speed (1x/2x/5x/10x), zoom (scroll wheel), pan (drag)

### Step-by-step capture workflow

**Preparation (~5 min):**
1. Open the showrunner output JSON. Print or display the `scenes[]` array as your shot list.
2. Load the simulation in playback mode: `http://localhost:3000/simulations/{sim_code}`
3. Set OBS to capture the browser window. Resolution: 1920x1080 (will crop to 1080x1920 for 9:16 in post).
4. Note the step numbers for each scene from the shot list.

**Capture (~10-15 min):**

Record each scene as a **separate clip**. This is easier than trying to nail the whole trailer in one take.

For each scene in the shot list:
1. Navigate to the scene's `step_range[0]` using the playback scrubber.
2. Set the camera: zoom to `camera.start_zoom`, position on `focus_persona`.
3. Set playback speed per the shot list.
4. Start OBS recording.
5. Play the scene. Manually adjust zoom/pan if the shot list specifies `end_zoom` different from `start_zoom`.
6. Stop recording at `step_range[1]`.
7. Name the clip: `scene_{scene_id}_{label}.mp4`

**For transitions:**
- **Scene cut / Fade to black / Card break:** Handle in post (FFmpeg/editor). Don't try to capture these.
- **Fly-over:** Record a continuous take: zoom out → pan across map → zoom in on new location. Do this between relevant scenes.
- **Time lapse:** Set playback to 10-25x, record the fast-forward segment.
- **Focus shift:** Record a continuous zoom/pan between two sprites in the same area.

**Capture settings:**
- Resolution: 1920x1080 minimum
- Frame rate: 30fps (sufficient for sprite animation)
- Format: MP4 (H.264) or MKV (for safety — MKV survives crashes)

### Alternative: Screenshot + Ken Burns (even simpler MVP)

If screen recording is too cumbersome, capture **key frame screenshots** and apply slow zoom/pan animation (Ken Burns effect) in post:

1. Navigate to each scene's key step. Screenshot.
2. In FFmpeg or a video editor, apply 3-5 second slow zoom on each screenshot.
3. Sequence screenshots with crossfade transitions.
4. Overlay narration + music.

This produces a documentary-still feel that actually works well with the warm narrator tone. Less dynamic, but faster to produce.

### FFmpeg compositing commands

**Step 1: Concatenate scene clips with transitions**

```bash
# Create a concat file
cat > concat.txt << 'EOF'
file 'raw/scene_1_hook.mp4'
file 'raw/scene_2_setup.mp4'
file 'raw/scene_3_development.mp4'
file 'raw/scene_4_turn.mp4'
file 'raw/scene_5_close.mp4'
EOF

# Concatenate (with crossfade between clips)
ffmpeg -f concat -safe 0 -i concat.txt \
  -filter_complex "
    [0:v][1:v]xfade=transition=fade:duration=0.5:offset=7.5[v01];
    [v01][2:v]xfade=transition=fade:duration=0.5:offset=19.5[v012];
    [v012][3:v]xfade=transition=fade:duration=0.3:offset=37.5[v0123];
    [v0123][4:v]xfade=transition=fade:duration=0.5:offset=49.5[vout]
  " -map "[vout]" -c:v libx264 -crf 18 output/video_only.mp4
```

**Step 2: Mix audio (narration + music + SFX)**

```bash
# Duck music under narration (sidechain-style)
ffmpeg -i audio/narration.mp3 -i audio/music_intrigue.mp3 \
  -filter_complex "
    [1:a]volume=0.3[music_low];
    [0:a][music_low]amix=inputs=2:duration=longest:dropout_transition=2[aout]
  " -map "[aout]" output/audio_mixed.mp3
```

**Step 3: Combine video + audio + subtitles**

```bash
ffmpeg -i output/video_only.mp4 -i output/audio_mixed.mp3 \
  -i cards/title_card.png \
  -filter_complex "
    [0:v]scale=1920:1080[v];
    [v][2:v]overlay=0:0:enable='between(t,58,60)'[vout]
  " \
  -map "[vout]" -map 1:a \
  -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -c:a aac -b:a 192k \
  -t 60 output/trailer_16x9.mp4
```

**Step 4: Crop to 9:16 for social**

```bash
# Center crop to vertical (keep center 607px width from 1080p)
ffmpeg -i output/trailer_16x9.mp4 \
  -filter_complex "crop=607:1080:656:0,scale=1080:1920" \
  -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -c:a copy \
  output/trailer_9x16.mp4
```

**Note:** These are baseline commands. For the MVP, a simple video editor (DaVinci Resolve free, CapCut) may be faster than FFmpeg scripting. The FFmpeg path is for future automation.

## 12. Mood Modes

The showrunner selects one mood per trailer based on the day's dominant emotional arc. Each mood affects music, narration tone, pacing, and transition preferences.

### Intrigue (default)

**When:** The day's most interesting thread involves mystery, hidden motives, social maneuvering, or quiet revelation.
**Narrator tone:** Curious, slightly conspiratorial. Knows things. Shares them sparingly.
**Pacing:** Measured. Deliberate pauses. Let things land.
**Music:** `intrigue_01` — sparse piano, subtle pulse.
**Preferred transitions:** Focus shift, card break, slow fly-over.
**Card text style:** Questions. "Who knew?" / "A quiet lie." / "3:00 PM."
**Example logline:** "Katya planned a quiet afternoon. The afternoon planned otherwise."

### Drama

**When:** Conflict, confrontation, relationship rupture, high-stakes decision, emotional breakdown.
**Narrator tone:** More urgent. Shorter sentences. Empathetic but unflinching.
**Pacing:** Faster cuts in development. Hard stop-down before the turn.
**Music:** `drama_01` — building strings, percussion, tension.
**Preferred transitions:** Scene cut (hard), silence drop, fade to black.
**Card text style:** Statements. "Trust broken." / "No going back." / "The truth."
**Example logline:** "Gosha said what he'd been holding in for days. Katya wished he hadn't."

### Wholesome

**When:** Friendship, kindness, humor, small victories, sweet moments, community warmth.
**Narrator tone:** Warmest. Genuine affection. A smile in the voice.
**Pacing:** Relaxed. Longer scenes. Room to breathe.
**Music:** `wholesome_01` — acoustic guitar, warm piano.
**Preferred transitions:** Fly-over (the village feels alive), time lapse (pleasant routine), gentle fades.
**Card text style:** Observations. "Small things." / "Tuesday." / "A good day."
**Example logline:** "Some days in the Ville, nothing extraordinary happens. Those are the best days."

## 13. Subtitle & Card Style Guide

### Text cards (card break transitions)

- **Font:** Inter, Helvetica Neue, or similar clean sans-serif
- **Size:** Large (fills ~40% of frame width)
- **Color:** White text, no background (over gameplay), or white text on semi-transparent dark overlay
- **Alignment:** Center
- **Animation:** Fade in over 0.3s. Hold 1.5-2s. Fade out 0.3s.
- **Maximum words:** 5
- **Case:** Sentence case. Not ALL CAPS (too aggressive for the tone).

### Dialogue subtitles

- **Font:** Same as cards but smaller (~60% of card size)
- **Position:** Lower third (bottom 20% of frame)
- **Color:** White with thin black outline (2px) for readability over any background
- **Attribution:** Show speaker name in slightly dimmer color above the line
- **Quotation marks:** Yes — distinguish dialogue from narration cards

### End card

- **Layout:** Centered. Day number large, village name smaller below.
- **Example:**
  ```
  DAY 17
  The Ville
  ```
- **Duration:** 2 seconds
- **Background:** Fade to black, then white text fades in

## 14. Quality Checklist

Run through this before exporting the final trailer.

### Story
- [ ] Hook is the most surprising/loaded moment, shown without context
- [ ] Protagonist is named in the first narrator line
- [ ] Dramatic irony present (viewer knows something character doesn't)
- [ ] Story does NOT fully resolve — ends on open question or quiet image
- [ ] One "devastating observation" line from the narrator
- [ ] Logline would make someone curious in one sentence

### Technical
- [ ] Total duration: 58-62 seconds
- [ ] Narration word count: 120-150 words
- [ ] At least one 2-3 second silence (visual-only beat)
- [ ] Maximum 2 dialogue excerpts as text overlay
- [ ] Maximum 3 subtitle cards (including end card)
- [ ] Music ducked under narration (voice always clearly audible)
- [ ] No clipping or audio distortion
- [ ] End card present and readable

### Pacing
- [ ] No scene longer than 18 seconds
- [ ] Speed ramps only during transit/routine, never during emotional moments
- [ ] Fly-over transition used maximum once
- [ ] Stop-down (near-silence) happens at the turn (~0:38-0:42)
- [ ] Energy builds from setup through development, drops at turn, resolves at close

### Social readiness
- [ ] 9:16 vertical crop exists and key elements are visible in crop
- [ ] Subtitles readable at mobile size
- [ ] First 3 seconds would stop a thumb-scroller (compelling visual or text)

## 15. Post-MVP: Director API Requirements

The biggest MVP bottleneck is manual screen recording (Step 4). To fully automate trailer generation, the frontend needs a **Director API** — a programmatic camera scripting interface.

### What to build (FE team inquiry)

The simulation frontend needs these new capabilities:

**A. Camera Scripting API**

Expose a `window.__executeCameraScript(script)` function (mirroring the existing `window.__executeMovementsForStep` pattern) that accepts:

```typescript
interface CameraDirective {
  timestamp_ms: number;       // When to execute (relative to playback start)
  action: 'zoom' | 'pan' | 'follow' | 'unfollow' | 'reset';
  target?: { x: number; y: number } | string;  // Tile coords or persona name
  zoom_level?: number;        // 0.5 - 2.0
  duration_ms?: number;       // Tween duration
  easing?: 'linear' | 'ease-in' | 'ease-out' | 'ease-in-out';
}

interface CameraScript {
  directives: CameraDirective[];
  playback_speed?: number;    // Default 1
}
```

**B. Canvas Video Export**

Add a recording mode using the browser's `MediaRecorder` API on the Phaser canvas:

```typescript
interface RecordingOptions {
  start_step: number;
  end_step: number;
  playback_speed: number;
  camera_script: CameraScript;
  resolution: { width: number; height: number };
  fps: number;
  format: 'webm' | 'mp4';
}

// Returns a Blob URL to the recorded video
window.__recordPlayback(options: RecordingOptions): Promise<string>
```

**C. Batch Scene Rendering**

For trailer assembly, expose a function that renders multiple scenes:

```typescript
interface SceneSpec {
  scene_id: number;
  start_step: number;
  end_step: number;
  camera_script: CameraScript;
  playback_speed: number;
}

// Returns array of Blob URLs, one per scene
window.__renderScenes(scenes: SceneSpec[]): Promise<string[]>
```

**D. Transition Rendering**

Built-in transition effects:

```typescript
type TransitionType = 'fade_to_black' | 'fly_over' | 'card_break';

interface TransitionSpec {
  type: TransitionType;
  duration_ms: number;
  params?: {
    card_text?: string;           // For card_break
    from_pos?: {x: number, y: number};  // For fly_over
    to_pos?: {x: number, y: number};    // For fly_over
  };
}
```

### Implementation priority

1. **Camera Scripting API** — unlocks programmatic camera control (highest value)
2. **Canvas Video Export** — enables automated video capture
3. **Batch Scene Rendering** — convenience wrapper over 1 + 2
4. **Transition Rendering** — nice-to-have; can be done in FFmpeg post for now

### Estimated scope
- Camera scripting: extends existing `CameraController.ts` with public methods
- Video export: `MediaRecorder` on Phaser canvas (~200 lines)
- Both could be behind a `?director=true` query param to avoid affecting normal playback

## Appendix B: Narrator Reference Voices

### Primary references

**1. David Attenborough**
- *Why:* The cadence. Every sentence has weight. Pauses are intentional. Treats the mundane as extraordinary.
- *Listen to:* Any segment of Planet Earth where he narrates animal behavior — the parallel to "narrating persona behavior" is almost exact.
- *Borrow:* Pacing, word emphasis, reverence for subject.
- *Avoid:* The very slow pace — Double trailers need slightly more energy.

**2. Jim Dale (Pushing Daisies)**
- *Why:* Omniscient narrator who knows every character's secrets and reveals them with a storyteller's instinct. Warm, whimsical, precise.
- *Listen to:* Pushing Daisies S1E1 opening narration. Note how he states facts about characters that feel like plot twists.
- *Borrow:* The omniscient reveal technique, personality, playfulness.
- *Avoid:* The storybook register may be too whimsical for drama mode — calibrate per mood.

**3. Morgan Freeman (March of the Penguins / Shawshank Redemption narration)**
- *Why:* Trust and warmth without condescension. The listener immediately feels in safe hands.
- *Listen to:* March of the Penguins opening. Note the simplicity of language.
- *Borrow:* The trust, the simplicity, the warmth.
- *Avoid:* Freeman's voice is very associated with his person — don't try to imitate; borrow the *quality*.

### Secondary references

**4. Ron Howard (Arrested Development)**
- *Why:* Deadpan omniscient narration with comic timing. "Michael had made a huge mistake."
- *Borrow for:* Wholesome mode. Humor through understatement.

**5. Werner Herzog (documentaries)**
- *Why:* Philosophical weight. Makes the ordinary feel cosmically significant.
- *Borrow for:* Intrigue mode. The feeling that something profound is happening beneath the surface.

### The Double voice = intersection

```
Attenborough's cadence
  + Jim Dale's omniscient playfulness
  + Freeman's warmth
  = The canonical Double narrator
```

Calibrate per mood:
- **Intrigue:** More Attenborough + Herzog. Measured. Observational.
- **Drama:** More Freeman. Empathetic. Direct. Simple words, heavy meaning.
- **Wholesome:** More Jim Dale + Ron Howard. Warmer. A smile in the voice. Gentle wit.
